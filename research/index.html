Research Group & PPI

About the research group

Our research group studies drug therapy for intractable vascular tumors and vascular malformations. It is supported by the Japan Agency for Medical Research and Development (AMED) and is led by Dr. Michio Ozeki, Department of Pediatrics, Gifu University Hospital (the “Ozeki group”). The group carries out research on the development of molecular targeted therapy using sirolimus (granules and tablets) for these conditions.

Working with many hospitals across Japan, the group conducted the investigator-initiated clinical trials that supported the approval of sirolimus for intractable lymphatic anomalies and, later, for a broader range of intractable vascular tumors and vascular malformations. The group also works to build registries, develop treatment guidelines, and connect patients with specialized care.

Patient and Public Involvement (PPI)

Patient and Public Involvement (PPI) means carrying out research with or by patients and members of the public, rather than only about or for them. Our research group actively promotes PPI: patients and family members contribute their perspectives so that research and information better reflect the needs of the people who live with these conditions.

This website itself is one result of that collaboration. Our PPI members work together with the research group in several roles, including:

  • Helping to plan and create the website and educational videos;
  • Reviewing the site’s content from the patient and family point of view to check that it is clear, accurate, and reassuring;
  • Contributing to planning and priority-setting for the group’s activities.

Through PPI, we aim to make sure that the information we publish and the research we pursue genuinely help patients and families affected by intractable vascular anomalies.

Interested in the group’s research outputs, clinical trials, and guidelines? See the For Healthcare Professionals page, or visit the Japanese official site for the most detailed and up-to-date information.
