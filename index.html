What are vascular anomalies?

“Vascular anomalies” is an umbrella term for a group of rare disorders in which blood vessels or lymphatic vessels form or grow abnormally. They are broadly divided into vascular tumors (such as infantile hemangioma and kaposiform hemangioendothelioma) and vascular malformations (such as capillary, lymphatic, venous, and arteriovenous malformations, and combined types). Some are mild and improve on their own, while others are intractable — causing pain, swelling, bleeding, infection, or organ dysfunction — and require specialized, multidisciplinary treatment.

In recent years, drug therapy with sirolimus, an mTOR inhibitor, has changed the treatment landscape for many intractable vascular anomalies. Our research group led the investigator-initiated clinical trials in Japan that supported its regulatory approval, and we continue to study how these medicines can be used safely and effectively.

Important: The information on this site is for general educational purposes and does not replace advice from your doctor. Diagnosis and treatment of vascular anomalies should always be decided together with specialists who know your individual situation.
